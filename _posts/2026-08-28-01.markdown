# 28 August 2026 
My previous attempt at building a website was asking Claude to do it. Original, I know, but I was curious and I thought Claude could remove the barrier to entry and kickstart the blog. I thought, "I'll never start if it's too hard". 

I knew what I wanted, Claude knew how to do it, and it automatically did it for me. In no time flat I had a website. That was easy. I didn't even have to think that hard. Time to start, right? 

Wrong. I had *zero* motivation to do anything with the website. I didn't even know why, but it  already lost its lustre. My brain dropped the project just as fast as I had dreamt it up, and it sat untouched for two weeks. 

Today I tried again, from scratch this time, and it has been a brutally slow process. Slow, full of roadblocks, and extremely fun. Something in my brain loves to be challenged, to learn, to try hard at something I suck at. It's likely the same part of my brain that loved DotA so much.. 

What I'm realising: 
- Trying hard at something proves I care, and caring means I will return to it.
- It truly is all about the journey.
- Claude is productivity tool, and you can't "produce" fun.

I'm trying to create the site using Github Pages, I've chosen Jekyll as the builder. I can't exactly tell you what I did, because I've been stumbling through. And I don't have the website up and running yet, (I'm writing this in Obsidian), because I'm roadblocked. My Macbook has insufficient storage space to install Developer Tools, which means I can't update Ruby via Homebrew, which means my "gems are the wrong version"???

*I don't even know what that means*, but I'm excited to figure that out. 

Good luck solving that, future me.
